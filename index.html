010:00 to 1:00 · 1 minute

Why this matters on our ward

Opening“A patient is fasting for ECT, their breakfast insulin is due, and they also receive a weekly weight-management injection. We need to know what each medicine does, what they have actually eaten, and what the anaesthetic plan says.”

On a mental health unit, appetite, engagement and meal timing can vary. Sedation or acute illness can make symptoms harder to recognise. A sudden change in behaviour or alertness warrants a BGL check alongside the usual clinical assessment.

By the end, staff should be able to recognise common GLP-1 medicines, distinguish insulin roles, and identify when fasting instructions need clarification.

021:00 to 3:30 · 2½ minutes

GLP-1 medicines: appetite, glucose and the stomach

These medicines increase insulin release when glucose is high, reduce glucagon and appetite, and can slow stomach emptying. They have a low hypoglycaemia risk alone. The risk increases when combined with insulin or a sulfonylurea such as gliclazide.

Examples for recognition. Verify the exact product and prescription. A patient may take one for weight management without having diabetes.

  • Before giving a dose: confirm the dose, scheduled day, last administration and any recent dose increase. Ask pharmacy about an uncertain or interrupted course.
  • Monitor: nausea, vomiting, bowel function, fluid intake and how much food the patient actually eats.
  • Escalate promptly: persistent vomiting, dehydration, severe or persistent abdominal pain, or constipation with pain, distension or vomiting.

For patients taking clozapine, continue bowel monitoring and escalate concerning symptoms. Clozapine has its own serious gastrointestinal hypomotility risk.

Procedure connection: stomach contents can remain despite usual fasting. Tell the anaesthetic team about GLP-1 treatment, including treatment prescribed outside the hospital.

Sources: TGA class safety update · Ozempic product information · Trulicity product information · Liraglutide product information · Mounjaro product information · TGA clozapine warning.

Type 1 diabetes: maintain basal insulin coverage. Omission can cause diabetic ketoacidosis, even when the patient is not eating. Obtain an explicit plan for any dose change. If a hypo is occurring when insulin is due, treat it immediately and obtain instructions for the dose and timing.

At the medication round

Check the patient, exact insulin, concentration, device, dose in units, BGL, recent doses and meal or fasting status. Follow local independent-check requirements. Pens are for one patient. Never withdraw insulin from a pen with a syringe.

Practical example“For NovoRapid, make sure the meal is available and the patient can eat before giving the prescribed mealtime dose. If they are refusing food, contact the prescriber for a plan. If insulin has already been given, act promptly to prevent hypoglycaemia.”

Timing differs between products. NovoRapid is generally given immediately before food. Actrapid generally requires food within 30 minutes. Follow the specific order and product instructions. A patient using an insulin pump needs a documented inpatient and procedural plan before any disconnection.

046:30 to 10:30 · 4 minutes

Plan fasting before ECT and other procedures

Fasting requires separate instructions for food, fluids, medicines and BGL monitoring. Confirm these before the fast starts. Routine ECT guidance commonly uses six hours for solids and two hours for clear fluids. The GLP-1 pathway adds preparation and changes the permitted fluids. Follow the documented anaesthetic instructions.

Current Australian GLP-1 advice

Routine elective withholding of GLP-1 or GIP/GLP-1 medicines is not recommended. The Australian consensus recommends continuing treatment with a 24-hour clear-fluid preparation, starting on waking the day before anaesthesia, followed by the specified fasting period. A missed weekly dose does not establish that aspiration risk has resolved.

Clear-fluid diet from waking, as ordered. No solid food.

Illustrative timing: ANZCA’s April 2026 clarification permits water only from six hours before induction, then nil by mouth for the final two hours. Any medication-with-water exception needs explicit instructions. “Sip Til Send” is currently excluded from this GLP-1 pathway.

During the earlier clear-fluid day: options include water, black tea or coffee, clear apple juice and clear cordial. Milk, smoothies and standard milk-based nutritional supplements are unsuitable. Agree the carbohydrate-containing fluids and insulin plan for patients with diabetes.

If the preparation was missed, broken or uncertain: notify the anaesthetic team before proceeding. Record what was consumed and when. Feeling well or having no nausea cannot confirm an empty stomach. The anaesthetist decides whether further assessment, a changed approach or postponement is needed.

While the patient is fasting

  • Use the written basal, mealtime and correction insulin orders.
  • For people with diabetes, confirm BGL targets and frequency. National perioperative guidance recommends hourly capillary BGLs while fasting in hospital, with limited exceptions.
  • Escalate delays, low or rising BGLs, vomiting or illness. Ask for ketone testing and medical review when indicated by the local pathway.

Across an ECT course

  • Arrange an individual diabetes and anaesthetic plan early. Seek an early place on the list.
  • Recheck intake, hydration and glucose trends before each treatment.
  • After recovery, confirm safe oral intake, arrange food promptly and coordinate prescribed insulin.

Sources: 2025 Australian GLP-1 consensus · ANZCA FAQs, including April 2026 clarification · ANZCA clear-fluid patient instructions · ADS–ANZCA diabetes guideline · Queensland ECT guideline, Appendix 2 · NSW ECT handbook, section 10.4.

0510:30 to 12:30 · 2 minutes

Recognise and treat hypoglycaemia promptly

BGL below 4 mmol/L triggers the local hypoglycaemia pathway. Look for sweating, shaking, hunger, confusion, irritability, unusual behaviour or drowsiness. Check BGL promptly when symptoms occur.

Alert, safe swallow, oral intake allowed

Give the local rapid-carbohydrate treatment, commonly 15 g. Recheck after 15 minutes and repeat according to the protocol until corrected. Provide follow-up carbohydrate when appropriate.

Nil by mouth or unsafe swallow

Call for help and follow the local IV glucose or IM glucagon pathway. Use the emergency response for reduced consciousness, seizures or deterioration. Never give oral treatment when swallowing is unsafe.

Treat the hypo immediately, even if ECT is delayed. Fasting must never delay treatment. Tell the anaesthetic team what was given and when.

Recheck BGL at the intervals in the local pathway after every treatment, and continue monitoring after recovery. Document the response and arrange review of the cause and subsequent insulin. Check for a missed meal, reduced intake, recent insulin, vomiting or a change in renal function.

Sources: National acute insulin chart and hypoglycaemia pathway · National non-acute insulin chart.

Two quick ward cases

Ask staff for the next action, then open the answer. Allow about one minute per case.

Case 1 · Type 1 diabetes, fasting for ECT

Glargine is due. BGL is 6.8 mmol/L. A colleague suggests omitting all insulin until the patient returns for breakfast. What do you do?

Show suggested response

Expected response: confirm the documented basal plan and maintain insulin coverage. If the plan is missing, contact the prescriber promptly. Check the separate mealtime insulin instructions and continue the prescribed BGL monitoring.

Basis: CEC insulin safety standard.

Case 2 · Weekly semaglutide and an ordinary dinner

A patient due for ECT at 08:00 ate a normal dinner at 18:00. They have fasted since midnight and feel well. Their last semaglutide dose was four days ago. Is preparation complete?

Show suggested response

Expected response: the GLP-1 clear-fluid preparation was not completed. Inform the anaesthetic team before proceeding and document the intake and medication history. Neither the overnight fast nor absence of nausea confirms an empty stomach.

Basis: 2025 Australian GLP-1 consensus.

0714:30 to 15:00 · 30 seconds

Three things to carry into the next shift

  1. Identify GLP-1 treatment early and confirm the anaesthetic preparation.
  2. Know which insulin dose covers which need. Protect basal coverage in type 1 diabetes.
  3. Check glucose and act on changes. Treat hypoglycaemia promptly and hand over the response.
Ward reference · Outside the timed presentation

Before transfer for ECT or a procedure

  • Diabetes type and all diabetes or weight-management medicines identified.
  • GLP-1 product, last dose, recent dose changes and gastrointestinal symptoms documented.
  • Actual last food, non-water fluid and water intake recorded.
  • Clear-fluid preparation confirmed when required. Any breach communicated.
  • Insulin and other diabetes medicine instructions documented, including the plan for delays.
  • Latest BGL, recent insulin, any hypoglycaemia and its treatment handed over.
  • Recovery meal, monitoring and medication arrangements confirmed.

Presenter preparation: have the ward’s hypoglycaemia pathway and ECT fasting instructions available. Confirm who provides the insulin plan, the required BGL schedule, ketone-testing triggers and the contact for anaesthetic clarification. These details depend on the local service.

The checklist is a practical synthesis of the linked guidance. For repeated ECT, agree an individual plan with the anaesthetic and diabetes teams. A clear-fluid preparation day also needs attention to nutrition and hydration.
